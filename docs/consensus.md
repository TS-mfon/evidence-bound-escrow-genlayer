# Consensus design

The leader and validators fetch every locked URL themselves. The comparative principle requires exact agreement on `verdict`, each criterion `decision`, `settlement_bps`, and the hash of fetched evidence. Explanations can differ only in wording. The contract independently enforces criterion weights, citation membership, settlement math, bounds, and state transitions. The initial review prompt contains no appeal context. A party may authorize exactly one appeal reason in stored case state; the subsequent review derives that reason from storage rather than accepting caller-supplied prompt text. `finalize_case` takes only `case_id`, preventing an arbitrary caller from injecting review context.

Recovery is deliberately separate from adjudication. An `OPEN` case can be refunded only by its sponsor after the seven-day submission deadline; the respondent cannot claim the escrow by withholding delivery. A `SUBMITTED` case remains protected from refund while all locked URLs are available. Once its review deadline passes, `recover_case` uses comparative consensus to independently classify the locked URLs; the first unanimous outage observation starts a three-day cure window, and a second observation must still include one of the originally unavailable URLs before the sponsor receives a refund. This prevents a party from bypassing a valid submitted delivery or manufacturing recovery from a temporary outage. An `APPEALED` case has a three-day appeal review deadline; after it expires, recovery restores the prior finalized review and routes through the normal settlement path. Every review is retained by sequence number, and terminal `SETTLED`/`RECOVERED` states cannot be reopened.
